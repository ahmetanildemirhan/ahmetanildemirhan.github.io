How are microchips made? — 7M+ views.

What is a chip, how is it made, and why is it so hard to make? For Interesting Engineering, I took the story from research and script all the way to edit, motion graphics and sound, and turned one of the most demanding manufacturing processes in the world into an explainer that reached 7M+ viewers.

The story

Everywhere, yet barely understood.

Microchips are in almost everything we use — phones, cars, game consoles — yet very few people know what they actually are or how they're made.

And making one is anything but simple: it's one of the hardest and most demanding manufacturing processes there is. That contrast — something so everyday, built in such an extraordinary way — is what makes the story so compelling.

The timing mattered too. The video came out while the world was in the middle of a chip shortage, and everyone was suddenly asking why chips were so hard to come by.

The goal was to answer three questions clearly enough for anyone to follow: What is a chip? How is it made? And why is it so hard?

My approach

Clear, visual, fast.

A clear structure

I researched the topic and built the script around those three questions — answering one at a time — so viewers always know where they are and what comes next.

Visual storytelling

Motion graphics carry the explanation: when the script talks about something you can't film, the animation shows it.

Tempo

Tight cuts and no dead air. Each chapter ends by pulling the viewer into the next one.

Sound that drives the pace

Music and sound design built to the edit, so transitions land and the energy never drops.
