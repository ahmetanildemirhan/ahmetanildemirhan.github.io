Case study · Science explainer

5 reasons the world ran out of chips — 7M+ views.

The global chip shortage was hitting everything from cars to game consoles, but almost nobody could explain why. For Interesting Engineering, I took the story from research and script all the way to edit, motion graphics and sound, turning a complex supply-chain crisis into a five-part explainer.

ClientInteresting Engineering
RoleEnd-to-end: research, script, edit, motion, sound
FormatExplainer
PlatformsYouTube & social
Reach7M+ views
The challenge

Chips are invisible.

Everyone felt the shortage — empty shelves, delayed cars, sold-out consoles — but the cause sits deep in a supply chain most people never see: factories, wafers, lead times and a handful of countries that make most of the world's chips.

The video had to make that abstract system concrete, and do it fast enough to hold a social-media audience that can scroll away at any second.

My approach

Clear, visual, fast.

A clear structure

I researched the story and wrote it as five numbered reasons — one idea per chapter — so viewers always know where they are and what comes next.

Visual storytelling

Motion graphics carry the explanation: when the script talks about something you can't film, the animation shows it.

Tempo

Tight cuts and no dead air. Each chapter ends by pulling the viewer into the next one.

Sound that drives the pace

Music and sound design built to the edit, so transitions land and the energy never drops.
